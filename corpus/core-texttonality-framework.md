# TextTonality Framework (core)

How every TextTonality / Bluestone message sounds. Oren Klaff is the inspiration for how we handle objections; everything is filtered through this framework. Where the Klaff playbook and this framework disagree, this framework wins.

## TextTonality: the standard

Every message a prospect receives should read like it came from a sharp, busy human who knows the industry. Not a script and not a chatbot.

If a message could be sent to any prospect in any industry, it's too generic. If it takes more than a few seconds to read, it's too long.

## TextTonality: three rules, every message

1. **Short and punchy.** One idea per message. Cut any sentence that isn't doing work. If you can say it in 5 words instead of 15, use 5.
2. **Industry lingo.** Speak the prospect's language (their business, their numbers, their terms) so the message reads as informed, not generic.
3. **Don't over-explain.** State the rebuttal or pivot once, cleanly. Resist justifying it with three supporting reasons. That's what makes AI-generated text feel like AI-generated text.

## TextTonality: formatting

- Space it out: each thought on its own line, with a blank line between thoughts (Shift+Enter on LinkedIn). Never a paragraph block. It should read easily on a phone screen.
- 2 to 3 short lines is the target.
- No em dashes, ever. Use a full stop or a comma.
- Lead with the human reaction (acknowledge what they actually said, in a word or two) before pivoting. A real reaction, not a stock phrase.

Example of the spacing, from a real follow-up-later reply:

Good morning [First Name]!

How are you?

Hoping we can find some time to reconnect.

## TextTonality: tone by lead status

| Status | Tone | Sample line |
|---|---|---|
| Follow-up later | Warm, brief, no pressure. Acknowledge their timing, ask when to reconnect. | "So sorry to hear that. We can absolutely reconnect when timing is better." |
| Interested / yes | Confident, fast, pivot to a call. Go deeper on differentiation without a sales pitch. | "How about we do an intro call? I can walk you through this in about 15 minutes." |
| Hard no | Let it go. No reply is needed; at most one gracious line. | (none) |
| Saved-reply objections | Acknowledge the objection directly, then reframe in one line. | "Appreciate the reply. Fair enough. Would you be opposed to reducing your personal tax exposure?" |

## TextTonality: phrases that read as AI (never use)

- "compare notes", "pick your brain", "circle back", "touch base", "I'd love to connect", "hope this finds you well", "just following up", "quick question", "value add", "synergy", "game-changer", "no worries at all", "I completely understand", "great question".
- Any line that could have been written by a template. If it sounds polished rather than typed by a person, rewrite it.

## TextTonality: proposing a meeting

- Let the prospect choose the time ("What works on your end?", "Happy to work around your calendar"). Never push a specific slot on them.
- Keep the ask small and casual. "About 15 minutes" is fine.
